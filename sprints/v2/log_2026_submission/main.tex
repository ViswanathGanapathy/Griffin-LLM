\begin{abstract}
Griffin is a relational tabular foundation model that encodes multi-table databases as heterogeneous graphs and applies a shallow message-passing neural network (MPNN) over per-target subgraphs, feeding the resulting row embeddings to a frozen in-context tabular predictor such as TabPFN or TabICL. Like most graph neural networks, Griffin cannot be stacked deeper than a few layers without suffering from over-smoothing, and its performance saturates at four message-passing layers. The Scalable Message Passing Neural Network (SMPNN) recipe imports three depth-scaling tricks from the Transformer literature---per-sub-block LayerNorm, sequential residual composition, and a DiT-style near-identity residual initialisation via a learnable~$\alpha$ scalar---and has been shown to enable substantially deeper stacks on homogeneous graph benchmarks. It is natural to ask whether these techniques carry over to Griffin's foundation-model setting and, if so, whether they yield gains that transfer across heterogeneous task families. We adapt SMPNN to Griffin's heterogeneous relational MPNN operator by inserting two sequential sub-blocks (graph convolution then feed-forward) within each Griffin layer, each with its own affine LayerNorm, and by placing the learnable $\alpha$ on the feed-forward sub-block so that at initialisation the whole layer acts as an identity mapping over the pretrained vanilla checkpoint. We evaluate the resulting architecture on the RelBench benchmark across four task families, two modern in-context heads, and multiple transfer directions, all reported under five random seeds. The results are more nuanced than a one-line "deeper is better" story: SMPNN's advantage over vanilla Griffin is domain-specific rather than universal, its most robust practical benefit is a large reduction in seed-to-seed variance rather than a mean improvement, and the effective depth ceiling shifts only modestly upward before over-smoothing sets in again. We also demonstrate directly that a decisive-looking single-seed pilot result reversed under multi-seed replication, and we argue that architectural comparisons on relational transfer tasks require an $n \ge 5$ seed floor to be trustworthy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Relational databases dominate enterprise data, yet most tabular ML operates on flat single tables. A recent line of work argues that this framing loses signal: foreign-key relationships between tables are semantically rich features that a graph encoder can exploit. \emph{Relational foundation models}---Griffin~\citep{wang2025griffin}, 4DBInfer, and the RelBench benchmark suite~\citep{robinson2024relbench}---treat a multi-table schema as a heterogeneous graph and apply message passing to learn representations that transfer across databases and tasks. Downstream prediction is typically delegated to a modern tabular in-context learner such as TabPFN~\citep{hollmann2024tabpfn} or TabICL~\citep{qu2024tabicl} that consumes the frozen encoder embeddings and produces per-row predictions via meta-learned attention over labelled context. The result is a two-stage pipeline: a general-purpose graph encoder feeds a general-purpose tabular predictor, with no per-task fine-tuning required.

Griffin's default encoder is a shallow ($L{=}4$) relational message-passing neural network (RMPNN) with mean+max aggregation per (target, edge-type) pair, gated bidirectional message flow, and a single shared non-affine LayerNorm. This design is intentionally conservative: attempts to stack it deeper hit the classical GNN over-smoothing pathology, where node representations converge exponentially fast in depth~\citep{oono2020oversmoothing} and lose discriminative information. In language modelling the analogous depth-instability problem was resolved by residual connections, per-sub-block LayerNorm, and---most recently in Diffusion Transformers~\citep{peebles2023dit}---by a learnable per-layer scalar~$\alpha$ initialised near zero, so that every layer starts as an approximate identity function and only "wakes up" as its gradient becomes useful. Whether the same trick ports to relational GNNs is an open question: the over-smoothing pathology of GNNs is not identical to the training instability of deep Transformers, even when the fix looks superficially similar.

The Scalable Message Passing Neural Network (SMPNN) recipe applies this analogy directly: two sequential sub-blocks per layer (graph convolution then feed-forward), each with its own affine LayerNorm, and a learnable $\alpha$ on the FFN sub-block. The mathematical delta from vanilla Griffin is small---roughly~12K additional parameters on a 40M-parameter model. The empirical question this paper asks is whether that small architectural change reliably improves \emph{transfer} performance for a relational foundation model that must generalise across heterogeneous task families, and how much of any apparent improvement survives multi-seed replication.

We ask three questions. \textbf{(Q1)} Does SMPNN improve in-distribution accuracy on Griffin's task set? \textbf{(Q2)} Does it improve cross-task transfer? \textbf{(Q3)} How many seeds are required for either answer to be reliable? A single-seed pilot in our early experiments suggested a decisive $+0.050$ SMPNN win on commerce transfer---a signal large enough to motivate a full multi-seed campaign. Under 5-seed replication that sign \emph{reversed} to $-0.030$, providing direct evidence that single-seed reporting for cross-task GNN transfer is systematically unreliable. This methodological finding sits alongside our architectural findings and is, we argue, the most portable contribution of the paper.

\paragraph{Contributions.} We contribute (i)~the first integration of SMPNN into a relational tabular foundation model ($<$0.03\% parameter overhead); (ii)~a rigorous $n{=}5$ evaluation of Griffin variants across 6 transfer directions and 2 ICL heads (240 test configurations); (iii)~evidence that SMPNN's benefit is \emph{domain-specific} and that its primary win is \emph{variance reduction}, not mean improvement; and (iv)~a reproducibility case study motivating $n\!\ge\!5$ as a minimum bar for relational-transfer claims.

\section{Griffin and SMPNN-Griffin}
\label{sec:arch}

\paragraph{Griffin encoder.}
For each target row (e.g.\ a user for whom we want to predict churn), Griffin samples a hop-2/fanout-20 subgraph of the surrounding relational neighbourhood and processes it with $L$~message-passing layers. The core operator (RMPNN) aggregates messages by (target-node, edge-type) group using a mean-then-max composition: mean pooling within an edge type is a variance-reducing aggregator over what may be a large fan-in; max pooling \emph{across} edge types then lets the model select the most informative relation per target row. Per-edge-type learnable embeddings gate the mean-pooled messages, giving the operator a relation-typed inductive bias without the manual per-relation module weaving of classical R-GCN or HAN architectures. Each vanilla layer applies a single residual update composed of three parallel branches---a feed-forward branch and two RMPNN branches (forward and reverse edges), each gated by zero-initialised per-node scalars---all sharing one non-affine LayerNorm:
\begin{equation}
  x^{(i+1)} = x^{(i)} + \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{ff}}\big(\mathrm{LN}(x^{(i)})\big) + g_{\text{fwd}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}(\cdot) + g_{\text{rev}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}_{\text{rev}}(\cdot). \label{eq:vanilla}
\end{equation}

At initialisation the two RMPNN branches contribute nothing ($g_{\text{fwd}}\!=\!g_{\text{rev}}\!=\!0$), so the layer is effectively a residual FFN. This is why the model trains stably at $L{=}4$. It is also why stacking beyond $L{=}4$ fails: the FFN branch runs unconditionally at coefficient 1, so noise from the still-training MP branches passes through the FFN and compounds across depth, with no learned mechanism to turn a layer off.

\paragraph{SMPNN-Griffin.}
We refactor each layer into two sequential sub-blocks with per-sub-block affine LayerNorms and a learnable residual scale~$\alpha$ on the FFN sub-block:
\begin{align}
  \tilde{x}    &= x^{(i)} + \mathrm{SiLU}\big(g_{\text{fwd}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}(\mathrm{LN}_1(x^{(i)})) + g_{\text{rev}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}_{\text{rev}}(\cdot)\big) \\
  x^{(i+1)}    &= \tilde{x} + \alpha_i \cdot \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{ff}}(\mathrm{LN}_2(\tilde{x})).                                                                     \label{eq:smpnn}
\end{align}
Three architectural changes distinguish Eq.~\eqref{eq:smpnn} from Eq.~\eqref{eq:vanilla}: (a)~$\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{pre}}$ inside the RMPNN is bare Linear with SiLU moved \emph{outside} the aggregation---combined with zero-initialised gates, sub-block~1 outputs $\mathrm{SiLU}(0)\!=\!0$ at step~1 and contributes nothing; (b)~$\alpha_i$ is initialised at a small value ($10^{-6}$ or $10^{-2}$), so the FFN sub-block is near-identity at init; (c)~the whole layer therefore acts as an identity function at initialisation, matching the DiT-style init that reliably enables 100+ Transformer layers in diffusion modelling. Total added parameters: two affine LNs and one $\alpha$ scalar per layer, ${\sim}12{,}300$ for a 40M-parameter Griffin.

\paragraph{Design rationale.}
Two design decisions require justification. \emph{Why sequential sub-blocks?} Vanilla Griffin's parallel-branch design forces the MP and FFN branches to compete: their gradients enter the same residual sum, and the FFN's uncontrolled contribution overwhelms the slowly-growing MP signal at deeper stacks. Sequential composition breaks this competition---the FFN sees a post-MP $\tilde{x}$ and can learn corrections rather than competing contributions. \emph{Why a learnable $\alpha$ instead of a fixed small scalar?} A fixed scale of $10^{-6}$ would forever bottleneck the FFN's effective contribution. Making $\alpha$ learnable lets each layer discover its own useful magnitude during training, while the small init preserves the initial-identity property for gradient flow.

\paragraph{$\alpha$ as a hyperparameter.}
We evaluate two initial values, motivated by the following intuition. $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ (paper default) starts the FFN sub-block essentially as a no-op; training must grow $\alpha$ substantially before the sub-block matters. $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ starts the FFN with a small but non-negligible contribution. When Griffin is \emph{warm-started} from a pretrained vanilla checkpoint (as in all our transfer experiments), $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ gives the FFN room to refine the already-learned representation from step~1, whereas $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ requires the model to first grow $\alpha$ into a useful range. Section~\ref{sec:results} shows this intuition holds on the direction where SMPNN helps but not universally.

\paragraph{Related work.}
Relational GNNs (R-GCN, HAN, HGT) share the (target, edge-type)-typed aggregation pattern that Griffin's RMPNN operationalises; Griffin's novelty is training at foundation-model scale across many databases jointly. Tabular foundation models (TabPFN~\citep{hollmann2024tabpfn}, TabICL~\citep{qu2024tabicl}) provide the downstream head; we take their embedding-consumption interface as given and ask about the upstream encoder. GNN depth-scaling techniques (PairNorm, GRAND, DropEdge, SIGN) have been evaluated primarily on single-graph node-classification, rarely under transfer; our results suggest their gains do not automatically carry over to the foundation-model regime. DiT-style near-identity init~\citep{peebles2023dit} is one of a family (LayerScale, ReZero, Fixup) that reliably enable ${>}100$-layer Transformers; SMPNN adapts the idea to relational GNNs.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
RelBench joint-v65, 24~tabular-relational tasks grouped into 4 families of 6: \emph{others-1} (sports, social, biology), \emph{others-2} (travel, clinical), \emph{commerce-1} (e-commerce), \emph{commerce-2} (marketplace, ads). Direction averages are over classification (AUROC) tasks only; regression (MAE) tasks are excluded from averages due to scale incompatibility. Full task lists in Appendix~\ref{app:detail}.

\paragraph{Backbones and training.}
Four architectures: \textbf{Vanilla-4} (baseline), \textbf{Vanilla-6} (depth control), \textbf{SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$} (D1, paper default), \textbf{SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$} (D3, warm-start optimised). All 20~backbones (4~$\times$~5~seeds) warm-start from a pretrained Vanilla-4 checkpoint. Training: 20~epochs, batch~256, AdamW at $\text{lr}{=}3\text{e}{-4}$, wd~$4\text{e}{-4}$, hop$=$2, fanout$=$20, hidden dim~512. No warmup or LR schedule.

\paragraph{ICL heads and seeds.}
Griffin's 512-d embeddings feed raw (no projection) into TabPFN\,v3 (30K context, 8-est.) or TabICL\,v2 (10K context, 8-est.), both frozen. We use 5~seeds (42--46) for all transfer directions and 3~seeds for the depth sweep, giving 240 test configurations. Compute cost: ${\sim}500$ H100-GPU hours. $n{=}5$ was chosen so that a claimed effect of ${\pm}0.05$ is detectable at $p{<}0.05$ under a paired t-test given Vanilla's per-seed std of~${\pm}0.035$ on commerce.

Three patterns are immediately visible.

\textbf{(i) Domain specificity.} SMPNN helps forward others-transfer (o1$\to$o2) but hurts every direction that originates from or targets a commerce family. On c2$\to$c1 the SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ loss is $\Delta = -0.022$, same-sign across all 5~seeds---decisive in the wrong direction. On c1$\to$c2, Vanilla-4 wins by $+0.023$ over SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$; the losing SMPNN configuration is not rescued by dropping $\alpha$ to $10^{-6}$ ($\Delta = -0.030$) or by moving to Vanilla-6 ($\Delta = -0.017$). The plausible mechanism is graph-structural: commerce tasks (clickthrough, conversion, sales) produce sparse transactional graphs with few-hop entity relationships, whereas the SMPNN residual encourages the model to weight frequency components of the graph signal---useful when the graph has rich spectral structure but potentially disruptive when the graph is structurally simpler and a fixed residual is a safer prior.

\textbf{(ii) Variance reduction is the primary win.} On o1$\to$o2 the mean improvement of SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ over Vanilla-6 is only~$+0.003$, but the standard deviation drops $7\times$ ($\pm 0.0102 \to \pm 0.0015$)---and $44\times$ vs Vanilla-4 ($\pm 0.0656$). Concretely, Vanilla-4 across seeds 42--46 produces $\{0.709, 0.867, 0.870, 0.827, 0.803\}$: a range of $0.161$ AUROC that spans well beyond any plausible effect size. SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ produces $\{0.862, 0.860, 0.860, 0.861, 0.858\}$: a range of $0.004$, an order of magnitude smaller than the effect it claims to detect. This variance-reduction is what makes the SMPNN claim defensible at small seed counts; the mean improvement alone would be within noise for any $n\!<\!20$.

\textbf{(iii) Directionality on others.} o1$\to$o2 favours SMPNN; o2$\to$o1 favours vanilla. The $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ variant is notably less stable on o2$\to$o1 ($\pm 0.0172$) than on o1$\to$o2 ($\pm 0.0015$)---an 11$\times$ swing in variance across a change of source family alone. This asymmetry rules out any simple story of "SMPNN is a strictly better initialiser": the same architecture with the same $\alpha$ helps in one direction and hurts in the other. The source--target pair (not the target family alone, and not the source family alone) is the operative unit of analysis for architectural choices in this setting.

\paragraph{Depth (Appendix~\ref{app:depth}).}
Both architectures peak at $L{=}6$ with SMPNN reaching the lowest variance of any configuration tested ($\pm 0.0012$). SMPNN needs $L{\geq}4$ to be useful: at $L{=}2$ it underperforms vanilla by $-0.107$---the $\alpha$-gated FFN interferes with too-shallow representations that cannot yet express useful spectral filtering. Both architectures degrade at $L{=}8$, consistent with over-smoothing setting in regardless of the DiT-style init. SMPNN delays but does not eliminate the pathology; the depth ceiling shifts by two layers, not more. Practical recommendation: for others-domain deployment, SMPNN at $L{=}6$ dominates all other configurations by both mean and variance criteria; there is no reason to go deeper.

\paragraph{Reproducibility case study.}
A single-seed pilot showed SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ winning c1$\to$c2 by $+0.050$---a decisive signal that motivated the full multi-seed campaign. Under 5-seed replication the sign of that effect \emph{reversed}: Vanilla-4 wins the same direction by $+0.030$. The seed-42-only reading was $0.607$; the 5-seed mean was $0.557 \pm 0.035$. High seed variance on commerce tasks makes any single-seed conclusion in this range indistinguishable from initialisation noise. The general lesson: for cross-task GNN transfer at moderate dataset sizes, effect sizes and initialisation variance are comparable in magnitude, so single-seed reporting is systematically unreliable regardless of the effect direction. We recommend $n\!\ge\!5$ as a floor and $n{=}10$ where compute allows.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results reframe how SMPNN should be understood in the relational-foundation-model setting. The Transformer-era depth-scaling tricks it imports \emph{do} raise the Griffin depth ceiling ($L{=}4 \to L{=}6$) and \emph{do} substantially reduce seed-variance on the tasks they help---but neither effect is universal. Commerce-domain graphs (sparse, transactional, few-hop) actively reject the spectral residual; $\alpha$~tuning does not rescue them, and additional depth also fails to help vanilla in that regime.

\paragraph{When to use which architecture.}
Vanilla-4 remains the right choice for commerce-domain tasks (both directions in our study), for compute-constrained deployments, and as a fair baseline for architectural comparisons. SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ is the right choice for others-domain tasks---especially when reporting under a small seed budget, where the variance-reduction is the decisive advantage even where the mean is comparable. When warm-starting from a pretrained vanilla checkpoint, $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ dominates $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ on the direction where SMPNN wins (o1$\to$o2). For random initialisation the paper-default $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ is the safer choice.

\paragraph{Connection to Transformer depth-scaling.}
The DiT-style init that reliably enables ${>}100$-layer Transformers gives only a two-layer depth boost in Griffin ($L{=}4 \to L{=}6$), and even that plateau is not universal across task families. This gap merits attention: the over-smoothing pathology of GNNs is not the same problem as Transformer training instability, even when the mathematical form of the fix appears similar. Transformer-era tricks may transfer partially, not universally, to GNN architectures, and each such transfer needs multi-seed empirical validation rather than being adopted by analogy.

\paragraph{Implications for community reporting norms.}
The most portable finding of this paper is methodological: on RelBench-style relational transfer at moderate dataset sizes, seed variance and effect size are comparable in magnitude. A $+0.050$ single-seed win can flip sign under 5-seed replication ($-0.030$ in our case). We therefore recommend $n\!\ge\!5$ seed reporting as the minimum bar for architectural claims on cross-task relational transfer, with explicit standard deviations, and encourage the LoG community to weight variance-reduction contributions as first-class results rather than as "no significant improvement."

\paragraph{Limitations.}
All conclusions are drawn from RelBench joint-v65; generalisation to other relational benchmarks is untested and is a natural follow-up on the broader 4DBInfer suite. All backbones warm-start from a vanilla checkpoint, so we cannot separate intrinsic SMPNN failure on commerce from a warm-start artefact---from-scratch training on commerce would resolve this. All cross-task transfer numbers use ICL heads (TabPFN, TabICL); a native-Griffin-head sanity check on o1$\to$o2 is in progress. The single-classification-task metric on o1$\to$o2 (airbnb-destination) is a narrow anchor; a second-anchor evaluation on rel-trial-study-outcome is in progress. Per-epoch $\alpha$ trajectory tracking to directly support the warm-start rationale is also in progress; the three follow-up results will land in the full technical report before camera-ready (see Appendix~\ref{app:pending}).

    $L$ & Vanilla mean & $\pm$Std   & SMPNN mean       & $\pm$Std   & $\Delta$      & Verdict                     \\
    \midrule
    2   & 0.8013       & $\pm 0.0913$ & 0.6948            & $\pm 0.0062$ & $-0.107^{**}$ & Vanilla wins decisively    \\
    4   & 0.8150       & $\pm 0.0920$ & \textbf{0.8488}   & $\pm 0.0114$ & $+0.034^{**}$ & SMPNN wins decisively      \\
    6   & 0.8543       & $\pm 0.0115$ & \textbf{0.8608}   & $\pm 0.0012$ & $+0.007$      & SMPNN wins (variance)      \\
    8   & 0.8009       & $\pm 0.0544$ & 0.8107            & $\pm 0.0849$ & $+0.010$      & Tie (both degrade)         \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The depth curve is non-monotone for both architectures. SMPNN requires $L{\ge}4$ to be useful (at $L{=}2$ the $\alpha$-gated FFN interferes with too-shallow representations; the model cannot express useful spectral filtering with only 2~layers). $L{=}6$ is the joint peak; SMPNN's $\pm 0.0012$ at $L{=}6$ is the lowest variance of any configuration tested in this study. Both degrade at $L{=}8$, indicating over-smoothing sets in regardless of the DiT-style init.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
For others-domain foundation-model deployment, SMPNN at $L{=}6$ dominates all other configurations by both mean and variance criteria. There is no reason to go deeper.

    \midrule
    o1$\to$o2  & TabPFN  & ---                     & 0.8602 $\pm 0.0015$        & ($\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ not run --- $10^{-2}$ selected as headline) \\
    c1$\to$c2  & TabICL  & 0.5276 $\pm 0.0244$     & 0.5348 $\pm 0.0279$        & $+0.007$ (within noise) \\
    c2$\to$c1  & TabICL  & 0.7005 $\pm 0.0151$     & 0.6974 $\pm 0.0120$        & $-0.003$ (within noise) \\
    o2$\to$o1  & TabPFN  & 0.7520 $\pm 0.0091$     & 0.7442 $\pm 0.0172$        & $-0.008$ ($\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ unstable) \\
    o2$\to$o1  & TabICL  & 0.7420 $\pm 0.0119$     & 0.7352 $\pm 0.0133$        & $-0.007$ ($\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ unstable) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
\end{table}

\paragraph{Reading.}
On the direction where $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ is decisively best (o1$\to$o2), it also has the tightest variance ($\pm 0.0015$). On directions where $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ is at parity or slightly worse (c1$\to$c2, c2$\to$c1), the $\alpha$ choice is within-noise. On the two o2$\to$o1 directions, $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ shows higher variance (up to $2\times$ $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$), suggesting $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ amplifies initialisation noise when the source--target topology is unfavourable.

\paragraph{Practical rule.}
For warm-started transfer to an others-domain target, use $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$. For warm-started transfer to any other target, $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ is the safer default.
