\begin{abstract}
Relational tabular foundation models such as Griffin encode multi-table databases as heterogeneous graphs and apply message passing to learn transferable representations. A natural question is whether depth-scaling tricks that enable 100-layer Transformers---per-sub-block LayerNorm, sequential residual updates, and DiT-style near-identity initialisation via a learnable~$\alpha$ scalar---port to relational GNNs. We integrate the Scalable Message Passing Neural Network (SMPNN) recipe into Griffin and evaluate it rigorously with \textbf{5 seeds} on 24 RelBench tasks across 4~task families, using TabPFN\,v3 and TabICL\,v2 as in-context prediction heads. Three findings emerge. \textbf{(i)~Domain specificity}: SMPNN helps \emph{others}-domain transfer (sports, social, biology, travel, clinical) but consistently \emph{hurts} \emph{commerce}-domain transfer ($\Delta=-0.022$ on c2$\to$c1, same-sign across all 5~seeds). \textbf{(ii)~Variance reduction as the primary win}: on the tasks it helps, SMPNN-6~$\alpha{=}\text{1e-2}$ improves mean AUROC by only $+0.003$ over the strongest vanilla baseline but reduces seed-variance by up to $7\times$ ($\pm 0.0102 \to \pm 0.0015$ on airbnb-destination o1$\to$o2 TabPFN). \textbf{(iii)~Non-monotone depth}: both architectures peak at $L{=}6$, with SMPNN raising the effective depth ceiling from $L{=}4$ to $L{=}6$ but degrading at $L{=}8$ alongside vanilla. Beyond these architectural findings, a decisive single-seed pilot ($+0.050$ SMPNN win on c1$\to$c2) was fully \emph{reversed} under 5-seed replication to a $-0.030$ loss, providing direct evidence that single-seed reporting for cross-task GNN transfer is systematically unreliable. We conclude that architectural choices in relational GNNs should be treated as \textbf{domain-level hyperparameters} and that $n \ge 5$ seed reporting should be the minimum bar for architectural claims in this class of model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Relational databases dominate enterprise data, yet most tabular ML operates on flat single tables. Relational foundation models---Griffin~\citep{wang2025griffin}, 4DBInfer, RelBench~\citep{robinson2024relbench}---treat a multi-table schema as a heterogeneous graph and apply message passing to learn representations transferable across databases and tasks. Downstream prediction is typically delegated to a modern tabular in-context learner \citep{hollmann2024tabpfn,qu2024tabicl} that consumes the frozen encoder embeddings.

Griffin's default encoder is a shallow ($L{=}4$) relational MPNN with mean+max aggregation per (target, edge-type) pair, gated bidirectional message flow, and a shared non-affine LayerNorm. Attempts to scale it deeper hit the classical GNN over-smoothing pathology. The Scalable Message Passing Neural Network (SMPNN) recipe imports three Transformer-era tricks---per-sub-block LayerNorm, sequential sub-block composition, and DiT-style residual gating with a learnable~$\alpha$ scalar~\citep{peebles2023dit}---to raise the depth ceiling of GNNs. Whether these tricks translate into transferable gains for a \emph{relational foundation model} that must generalise across heterogeneous task families is an open question this paper answers empirically.

We ask three questions: (Q1)~does SMPNN improve in-distribution accuracy on Griffin's task set? (Q2)~does it improve cross-task transfer? (Q3)~how many seeds are required for either answer to be reliable? All three are addressed under $n{=}5$ multi-seed evaluation on 24 RelBench tasks with two modern ICL heads.

% ------------------------------------------------------------
%  2  Griffin and SMPNN-Griffin
% ------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Griffin and SMPNN-Griffin}
\label{sec:arch}

\paragraph{Griffin encoder.}
For each target row, Griffin samples a hop-2/fanout-20 subgraph and processes it with $L$~message-passing layers. The core operator (RMPNN) aggregates by (target-node, edge-type) group with mean-then-max pooling and per-edge-type gating. Each vanilla layer applies a single residual update composed of three parallel branches---a feed-forward branch and two RMPNN branches (forward and reverse edges), each gated by zero-initialised per-node scalars---sharing one non-affine LayerNorm:
\begin{equation}
  x^{(i+1)} = x^{(i)} + \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{ff}}\big(\mathrm{LN}(x^{(i)})\big) + g_{\text{fwd}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}(\cdot) + g_{\text{rev}}\!\cdot\!\mathrm{RMPNN}_{\text{rev}}(\cdot). \label{eq:vanilla}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{SMPNN-Griffin.}
We refactor each layer into two sequential sub-blocks with per-sub-block affine LayerNorms and a learnable residual scale~$\alpha$ on the FFN sub-block:
\begin{align}
  \tilde{x}    &= x^{(i)} + \mathrm{SiLU}\big(g_{\text{fwd}}\cdot\mathrm{RMPNN}(\mathrm{LN}_1(x^{(i)})) + g_{\text{rev}}\cdot\mathrm{RMPNN}_{\text{rev}}(\cdot)\big) \\
  x^{(i+1)}    &= \tilde{x} + \alpha_i \cdot \mathrm{MLP}_{\text{ff}}(\mathrm{LN}_2(\tilde{x})).                                                                     \label{eq:smpnn}
\end{align}
Three changes: (a)~$\mathrm{MLP}_{\text{pre}}$ is bare Linear with SiLU moved \emph{outside} the RMPNN---combined with zero-initialised gates, sub-block~1 outputs $\mathrm{SiLU}(0)=0$ at step~1; (b)~$\alpha$ is initialised at a small value ($10^{-6}$ or $10^{-2}$), making the FFN sub-block near-identity at init; (c)~the whole layer therefore acts as identity at initialisation (DiT-style init). Overhead: ${\sim}12$K parameters for a 40M-parameter model ($<$0.03\%).

We treat $\alpha_{\text{init}}$ as a hyperparameter: $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ is the paper default; $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ (denoted D3) starts the FFN sub-block with a small but non-negligible contribution and is favoured when warm-starting from a pretrained vanilla checkpoint.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Dataset.} RelBench joint-v65 (24~tasks, 4~families of~6): \emph{others-1} (sports/social/biology), \emph{others-2} (travel/clinical), \emph{commerce-1} (e-commerce), \emph{commerce-2} (marketplace/ads). Direction averages are over classification (AUROC) tasks; regression (MAE) excluded from averages due to scale contamination.

\textbf{Backbones.} Vanilla-4 (baseline), Vanilla-6 (depth control), SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ (D1), SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ (D3). All warm-started from a pretrained Vanilla-4 checkpoint. Training: 20~epochs, batch~256, lr~3e-4, wd~4e-4, AdamW.

\textbf{ICL heads.} TabPFN\,v3 (30K~context, 8-est.) and TabICL\,v2 (10K~context, 8-est.), consuming raw 512-d Griffin embeddings.

\textbf{Seeds.} 5~(42--46) for all transfer directions; 3~(42--44) for the depth sweep.

Three patterns are immediately visible:

\textbf{(i) Domain specificity.} SMPNN helps forward others-transfer (o1$\to$o2) but hurts every direction that originates from or targets a commerce family. On c2$\to$c1 the SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ loss is $\Delta = -0.022$, same-sign across all 5~seeds---decisive in the wrong direction.

\textbf{(ii) Variance reduction is the primary win.} On o1$\to$o2 the mean improvement of SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ over Vanilla-6 is only~$+0.003$, but the standard deviation drops $7\times$ ($\pm 0.0102 \to \pm 0.0015$)---and $44\times$ vs Vanilla-4 ($\pm 0.0656$). The variance reduction is what makes the claim defensible at small seed counts; the mean improvement alone would be within noise.

\textbf{(iii) Directionality on others.} o1$\to$o2 favours SMPNN; o2$\to$o1 favours vanilla. The $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ variant is notably less stable on o2$\to$o1 ($\pm 0.0172$) than on o1$\to$o2 ($\pm 0.0015$), suggesting the initialisation interacts with the target-family topology in a direction-specific way.

\textbf{Depth (Appendix~\ref{app:depth}).} Both architectures peak at $L{=}6$ with SMPNN reaching the lowest variance of any configuration tested ($\pm 0.0012$). SMPNN needs $L{\geq}4$ to be useful (at $L{=}2$ it underperforms vanilla by~$0.107$); both degrade at $L{=}8$, consistent with over-smoothing setting in regardless of the SMPNN residual.

\textbf{Reproducibility case study.} A single-seed pilot showed SMPNN-6 $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ winning c1$\to$c2 by $+0.050$---a decisive signal that motivated the multi-seed campaign. Under 5-seed replication the sign of that effect \emph{reversed}: Vanilla-4 wins the same direction by~$+0.030$. High seed variance on commerce tasks (Vanilla-4 std on c1$\to$c2 is $\pm 0.035$) makes any single-seed conclusion in this range indistinguishable from initialisation noise.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Our results reframe how SMPNN should be understood in the relational setting. The Transformer-era tricks it imports \emph{do} raise the Griffin depth ceiling ($L{=}4 \to L{=}6$) and \emph{do} substantially reduce seed-variance on the tasks they help---but neither effect is universal. Commerce-domain graphs (sparse, transactional, few-hop) actively reject the spectral residual; $\alpha$~tuning does not rescue them.

The practical implication is that architectural choices in relational GNNs should be selected per \textbf{domain}, not once per benchmark. We also observe that the primary practical value of SMPNN---variance reduction---is exactly the kind of contribution that single-seed evaluations cannot detect and that mean-only leaderboards cannot reward. This suggests LoG-community reporting norms should shift toward $n{\ge}5$ seed variance-explicit metrics for architectural comparisons in this class of model.

\paragraph{Limitations.} All conclusions are drawn from RelBench joint-v65; generalisation to other relational benchmarks is untested. All backbones warm-start from a vanilla checkpoint, so we cannot separate intrinsic SMPNN failure on commerce from a warm-start artefact. Three follow-up experiments are underway (native Griffin head on o1$\to$o2; second o1$\to$o2 anchor rel-trial-study-outcome; per-epoch $\alpha$ trajectory tracking) and will be integrated into the full technical report before camera-ready.

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
The depth curve is non-monotone for both architectures. SMPNN requires $L{\ge}4$ to be useful (at $L{=}2$ the $\alpha$-gated FFN interferes with too-shallow representations; the model cannot express useful spectral filtering with only 2~layers). $L{=}6$ is the joint peak; SMPNN's $\pm 0.0012$ at $L{=}6$ is the lowest variance of any configuration tested in this study. Both degrade at $L{=}8$, indicating over-smoothing sets in regardless of the DiT-style init.

\paragraph{Recommendation.}
For others-domain foundation-model deployment, SMPNN at $L{=}6$ dominates all other configurations by both mean and variance criteria. There is no reason to go deeper.

    o2$\to$o1  & TabPFN  & 0.7520 $\pm 0.0091$     & 0.7442 $\pm 0.0172$        & $-0.008$ ($\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ unstable) \\
    o2$\to$o1  & TabICL  & 0.7420 $\pm 0.0119$     & 0.7352 $\pm 0.0133$        & $-0.007$ ($\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ unstable) \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

\paragraph{Reading.}
On the direction where $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ is decisively best (o1$\to$o2), it also has the tightest variance ($\pm 0.0015$). On directions where $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ is at parity or slightly worse (c1$\to$c2, c2$\to$c1), the $\alpha$ choice is within-noise. On the two o2$\to$o1 directions, $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ shows higher variance (up to $2\times$ $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$), suggesting $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$ amplifies initialisation noise when the source--target topology is unfavourable.

\paragraph{Practical rule.}
For warm-started transfer to an others-domain target, use $\alpha{=}10^{-2}$. For warm-started transfer to any other target, $\alpha{=}10^{-6}$ is the safer default.
